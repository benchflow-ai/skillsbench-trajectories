    14→Abstract
    15→Alignment-free sequence analysis approaches provide important alternatives
    16→over multiple sequence alignment (MSA) in biological sequence analysis because alignment-free approaches have low computation complexity and are
    17→not dependent on high level of sequence identity, however, most of the existing alignment-free methods do not employ true full information content
    18→of sequences and thus can not accurately reveal similarities and diﬀerences
    19→among DNA sequences. We present a novel alignment-free computational
    20→method for sequence analysis based on Ramanujan-Fourier transform (RFT),
    21→in which complete information of DNA sequences is retained. We represent
    22→DNA sequences as four binary indicator sequences and apply RFT on the
    23→indicator sequences to convert them into frequency domain. The Euclidean
    24→distance of the complete RFT coeﬃcients of DNA sequences are used as
    25→similarity measure. To address the diﬀerent lengths in Euclidean space of
    26→RFT coeﬃcients, we pad zeros to short DNA binary sequences so that the
    27→binary sequences equal the longest length in the comparison sequence data.
    28→Thus, the DNA sequences are compared in the same dimensional frequency
    29→space without information loss. We demonstrate the usefulness of the proposed method by presenting experimental results on hierarchical clustering of
    30→genes and genomes. The proposed method opens a new channel to biological
    31→sequence analysis, classiﬁcation, and structural module identiﬁcation.

   559→We scrutinize the effect of polyvalent ions on polymer-DNA interactions. We extend a recently
   560→developed test charge theory [S. Buyukdagli et al., Phys. Rev. E 94, 042502 (2016)] to the case of
   561→a stiff polymer interacting with a DNA molecule in an electrolyte mixture. The theory accounts for
   562→one-loop level electrostatic correlation effects such as the ionic cloud deformation around the strongly
   563→charged DNA molecule as well as image-charge forces induced by the low DNA permittivity. Our
   564→model can reproduce and explain various characteristics of the experimental phase diagrams for
   565→polymer solutions. First, the addition of polyvalent cations to the electrolyte solution results in
   566→the attraction of the negatively charged polymer by the DNA molecule. The glue of the like-charge
   567→attraction is the enhanced shielding of the polymer charges by the dense counterion layer at the
   568→DNA surface. Secondly, through the shielding of the DNA-induced electrostatic potential, mono- and
   569→polyvalent cations of large concentration both suppress the like-charge attraction. Within the same
   570→formalism, we also predict a new opposite-charge repulsion effect between the DNA molecule and a
   571→positively charged polymer. In the presence of polyvalent anions such as sulfate or phosphate, their
   572→repulsion by the DNA charges leads to the charge screening deficiency of the region around the DNA
   573→molecule. This translates into a repulsive force that results in the decomplexation of the polymer
   574→from DNA. This opposite-charge repulsion phenomenon can be verified by current experiments and
   575→the underlying mechanism can be beneficial to gene therapeutic applications where the control over
   576→polymer-DNA interactions is the key factor.
