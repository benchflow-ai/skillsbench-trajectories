14-Abstract
15-Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a widely used offline preference optimization algorithm that reparameterizes reward functions in reinforcement learning
16-from human feedback (RLHF) to enhance simplicity and training stability. In this
17:work, we propose SimPO, a simpler yet more effective approach. The effectiveness
18:of SimPO is attributed to a key design: using the average log probability of a
19-sequence as the implicit reward. This reward formulation better aligns with model
20-generation and eliminates the need for a reference model, making it more compute
21-and memory efficient. Additionally, we introduce a target reward margin to the
22:Bradley-Terry objective to encourage a larger margin between the winning and
23-losing responses, further improving the algorithm’s performance. We compare
24:SimPO to DPO and its recent variants across various state-of-the-art training setups,
25-including both base and instruction-tuned models such as Mistral, Llama 3, and
26-Gemma 2. We evaluate on extensive chat-based evaluation benchmarks, including
27:AlpacaEval 2, MT-Bench, and Arena-Hard. Our results demonstrate that SimPO
28-consistently and significantly outperforms existing approaches without substantially
29:increasing response length. Specifically, SimPO outperforms DPO by up to 6.4
30-points on AlpacaEval 2 and by up to 7.5 points on Arena-Hard. Our top-performing
31-model, built on Gemma-2-9B-it, achieves a 72.4% length-controlled win rate on
32-AlpacaEval 2, a 59.1% win rate on Arena-Hard, and ranks 1st on Chatbot Arena

1485:Preference optimization objectives. A variety of preference optimization objectives have been

1498-Conclusion
1499-
1500:In this work, we propose SimPO, a simple and effective preference optimization algorithm that
1501-consistently outperforms existing approaches across various training setups. By aligning the reward
1502:function with the generation likelihood and introducing a target reward margin, SimPO eliminates
1503-the need for a reference model and achieves strong performance without exploiting the length bias.
1504:Extensive analysis demonstrates that the key designs in SimPO are crucial and validates the efficiency
1505:and effectiveness of SimPO. A detailed discussion of the limitations can be found in Appendix A.

1509:DPO can be as memory efficient as SimPO if it were implemented to separate the forward passes of the
1510-reference model from the actual preference optimization. However, this implementation is not standard practice.

1826-Limitations
1827-
1828-More in-depth theoretical analysis. Despite the empirical success and intuitive motivation of
1829:SimPO, a more rigorous theoretical analysis is necessary to fully understand the factors contributing
1830-to its effectiveness. Additionally, we introduce an additional hyperparameter, the target reward
1831-margin, which requires manual tuning. Future work could explore how to determine the optimal
1832:margin automatically and provide a more theoretical understanding of SimPO.
1833:Safety and honesty. SimPO is designed to optimize the generation quality of language models
1834-by pushing the margin between the average log likelihood of the winning response and the losing
1835-response to exceed a target reward margin. However, it does not explicitly consider safety and honesty
1836-aspects, which are crucial for real-world applications. Future work should explore integrating safety
1837:and honesty constraints into SimPO to ensure that the generated responses are not only high-quality
1838-but also safe and honest. The dataset used in this work, UltraFeedback [23], primarily focuses on
1839-helpfulness, and future research may consider a more comprehensive study utilizing larger-scale
1840-preference datasets [43, 95] and evaluation benchmarks [81] that place a strong emphasis on safety
1841-aspects. Nonetheless, we observe that this method consistently achieves high TruthfulQA [57]
1842:performance compared to other objectives in Table 9, suggesting its potential for safety alignment.
1843-Performance drop on math. We observed that preference optimization algorithms generally
1844-decrease downstream task performance, particularly on reasoning-heavy tasks like GSM8k, as shown
1845:in Table 9. SimPO occasionally results in performance comparable to or worse than DPO. We
1846-hypothesize that this may be related to the choice of training datasets, hyperparameters used for
1847-training, or a mismatch of chat templates used for downstream task evaluations. One explanation is
1848:that the preference optimization objective may not be effectively increasing the likelihood of preferred
1849-sequences despite increasing the reward margin. [63] first observed this phenomenon and point out
1850-that this can hinder learning from math preference pairs where changing one token can flip the label
1851-(e.g., changing 2 + 2 = 4 to 2 + 2 = 5). They propose a simple regularization strategy to add back a
1852:reference-model calibrated supervised fine-tuning loss to the preference optimization objective, and
1853-effectively mitigate this issue. Future work may consider integrating this regularization strategy into
1854:SimPO to improve performance on reasoning-heavy tasks.
