The non-classical nature of the multiplexed photons is verified by mea-\nsuring the second-order correlation function with an average value of g(2)(0) = 0.060(13), indicating\nnegligible crosstalk between the multiplexed modes.\nIn addition, we characterize the motional\ndegree-of-freedom of the ion crystal after transport and find that it is coherently excited to as much\nas ¯nα ≈110 for the center-of-mass mode. Our proof-of-principle implementation paves the way\nfor large-scale quantum networking with trapped ions, but highlights some challenges that must be\novercome.\nRemote entanglement across distant quantum nodes [?\n? ] may be used for long-distance quantum key distribu-\ntion [? ? ], modular quantum computer architectures [?\n? ], as well as quantum enhanced metrology and sens-\ning [? ? ? ]. Light-matter quantum interfaces are a fun-\ndamental building block for such applications, and allow\nfor distributed entanglement between stationary matter\nqubits by using \"flying\" photons.\nFor practical purposes, these quantum interfaces need\nto be capable of establishing remote entanglement at\nhigh rates across a large-scale network of quantum nodes.\nHowever, in widely adopted probabilistic schemes based\non heralded photon detection [? ? ? ], it is infeasible to\ndistribute entanglement at useful rates over a few kilo-\nmeters, as the attempt rate is limited by the round-trip\ntravel-time of photons in a single mode. For example, for\na communication distance of 10 km, photon-travel time\nwill be around 100 µs, meaning that the attempt can-\nnot be higher than 10 kHz. In this scenario, even with\nuse of a state-of-the-art photon-matter interface that can\nyield close to unity photon extraction efficiency [? ], the\nmatter-photon entanglement rate beyond tens of kilome-\nters would be limited to sub 10 s−1 with total loss around\n15 dB after considering the collective probability of gener-\nating and detecting telecom photons at each node, along\nwith the optical fiber loss [? ? ].\nA key solution to this challenge is multiplexing: to\ncombine multiple signals into a single channel and there-\nfore increase the attempt rate [? ? ]. Multiplexing has\nbecome a mature technology for ensemble-based quan-\ntum interfaces, both in atomic gases and rare-earth ion\ndoped solid-state systems [? ? ? ? ]. However, large-\nscale local quantum information processing is technically\nchallenging in these platforms [? ].\nIn contrast, single emitters, including trapped ions and\nneutral atoms, offer excellent local quantum information\nprocessing capability beside their natural interface with\nlight at convenient wavelengths for quantum frequency\nconversion (QFC) [?\n?\n], and the possibility of long-\nlived storage of entanglement [? ? ]. On the other hand,\nimplementing a multiplexed light-matter interface with\nthese systems is technically challenging. Towards over-\ncoming this problem, a few multiplexing schemes have\nalready been proposed for ion and atom-based quantum\nprocessors [? ? ? ? ]. The only reported experimental\nwork, we are aware of, is the demonstration of multi-\nplexing using a static three-ion chain [? ]. In our experiments, we generate on-demand sin-\ngle photons by shuttling a nine-ion chain across the focus\nof a single-ion addressing beam.
