## Methodology

### Step 1: Data Cleaning
- Survey Data: 5,027 respondents cleaned and deduplicated
- Purchase Data: 438,050 transactions cleaned (removed duplicates, invalid dates, missing critical fields)
- Date Range: 2019-01-01 to 2020-12-31
- 243 unique product categories analyzed

### Step 2: Anomaly Detection
- Used counterfactual prediction based on historical trends (pre-March 2020)
- Calculated z-scores normalized to -100 to 100 scale
- Anomaly Index = (z-score / 3) × 100
  - +100: Very unusual increase in spending
  - 0: Normal spending
  - -100: Very unusual decrease in spending

### Step 3: Feature Engineering
- 41 demographic features engineered from survey data:
  - Age features (numeric, squared)
  - Income features (numeric, log-transformed)
  - Education level (ordinal encoding)
  - Gender, ethnicity, health status
  - Substance use indicators
  - Amazon usage frequency
  - Interaction features (high income × educated, young × high usage)

### Step 4: Causal Analysis - Difference-in-Differences (DiD)
- Compared treatment period (March 2020) vs baseline (Jan-Feb 2020)
- Analyzed both:
  - **Intensive Margin**: Average spending conditional on purchase
  - **Extensive Margin**: Probability of making a purchase
- Univariate DiD regression for each demographic feature
- Top 3 drivers identified per category per margin

## Causal Analysis Results

### Surge Categories - Key Drivers

**Intensive Margin** (Top 3 drivers for each category):
- Features explaining increased spending among purchasers
- Example: PROTECTIVE_GLOVE - health status and usage frequency

**Extensive Margin** (Top 3 drivers for each category):
- Features explaining increased purchase likelihood
- Example: PROTECTIVE_GLOVE - young, high-usage customers

### Slump Categories - Key Drivers

**Intensive Margin** (Top 3 drivers for each category):
- Features explaining reduced spending among purchasers
- Example: PLANNER - education level, income

**Extensive Margin** (Top 3 drivers for each category):
- Features explaining reduced purchase likelihood
- Example: APPAREL - demographics shifting away from fashion purchases

## Conclusions

1. **COVID-19 Impact**: March 2020 shows clear behavioral shifts aligned with pandemic onset
   - Surge: Essential items (protective equipment, cleaning, food)
   - Slump: Discretionary items (fashion, accessories, books)

2. **Demographic Drivers**: Different customer segments show varying responses
   - Health-conscious and high-usage customers drove surge categories
   - Income and education levels influenced slump categories

3. **Margin Effects**:
   - Extensive margin (purchase likelihood) showed stronger effects for surge categories
   - Intensive margin (spending conditional on purchase) showed mixed effects

4. **Policy Implications**: E-commerce platforms should prepare for:
   - Demand shifts during crisis periods
   - Supply chain challenges for essential items
   - Demographic targeting opportunities
